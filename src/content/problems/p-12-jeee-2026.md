## Problem Content \

\
Let
$$  \alpha =(1- 2 \cos{\frac{\pi}{11}})(1- 2 \cos{\frac{3\pi}{11}})(1- 2 \cos{\frac{9\pi}{11}})(1- 2 \cos{\frac{27\pi}{11}})(1- 2 \cos{\frac{81\pi}{11}}).  $$
Then the value of $5 - \alpha^2$ is:\

## Solution & Proof

**Method 1:** Since $ 1 - 2 \cos{2 \theta} = 3 - 4 \cos^2 \theta = \frac{3 \cos{\theta} - 4 \cos^3{\theta}}{\cos{\theta}}= \frac{- \cos{3 \theta}}{\cos{\theta}}.  $ \
The problem can be rephrased as follows. $$  \alpha = \prod_{k=0}^{4} \left(1- 2 \cos{\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{11}} \right) $$ $$  \alpha = \prod_{k=0}^{4} \left(\frac{-  \cos{\frac{3(2k+1)\pi}{22}} }{ \cos{\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{22}}} \right) $$ $$  = - \prod_{k=0}^{4} \left( \frac{ \cos{\frac{3(2k+1)\pi}{22}} }{ \cos{\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{22}}}  \right) = - \prod_{k=0}^{4} \left( \frac{ \cos{\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{22}} }{ \cos{\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{22}}}  \right)  $$ The above conversion is due to the fact that $ \cos{(\pi- \theta)} = - \cos{\theta}. $ This becomes a telescopic product. $$ \implies \alpha =  - \prod_{k=0}^{4} \left( \frac{ \cos{\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{22}} }{ \cos{\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{22}}}  \right) = 1.  $$

**Method:2**

If you are an explorer, then follow this method. This method by, virtue of being non-routine, is long but you will learn a lot. I had suggested this method to Dr. K D Joshi and he was glad to include it on his blog and give the credit.  Consider the equation $Z^{11}=-1$. The roots of the equation are as follows. $Z_{k}= \cos{(\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{11})} + i \sin{(\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{11})} $, where $ k \in {0,1,2, \dots, 10}$. For $k=5$,the root $Z_{5} = -1.$ The equation $ Z^{11}+1=0 $ can be factorised as:

$$ (Z+1)(Z^{10}-Z^{9}+ \dots +Z^2-Z+1) = 0 .   $$

Therefore, the roots of equation $ Z^{10}-Z^{9}+ \dots +Z^2-Z+1 = 0   $ are $ Z_{k}= \cos{(\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{11})} + i \sin{(\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{11})} $, where $ k \in {0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9, 10}$. Notice that $\Re(Z_{k})= \Re(Z_{11-k})$. So, the equation

$$ Z^{10}-Z^{9}+ \dots +Z^2-Z+1 = 0, \ \text{for} \ k \in \lbrace 0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9, 10 \rbrace \tag{A} $$

has ten roots, of which every pair has the same real part. Observe that

$$ Z_{11-k}=\overline{Z_k}, $$

so each conjugate pair determines the same value of

$$ x=Z+\frac1Z=2\Re(Z). $$

Hence the ten roots of equation $(A)$ give rise to exactly five distinct values of $x$, namely

$$ 2\cos\frac{\pi}{11}, 2\cos\frac{3\pi}{11}, 2\cos\frac{5\pi}{11}, 2\cos\frac{7\pi}{11}, 2\cos\frac{9\pi}{11}. $$

Since none of the roots of equation $(A)$ is zero, we may divide by $Z^5$ to obtain

$$   (Z^5+ \frac{1}{Z^5})- (Z^4+ \frac{1}{Z^4})+ (Z^3+ \frac{1}{Z^3}) - (Z^2+ \frac{1}{Z^2}) + (Z+ \frac{1}{Z}) -1 = 0  \tag{B}   $$ Set $  Z+\frac{1}{Z} = x. $ Since $Z = e^{i\theta } \implies  Z+\frac{1}{Z} = 2 \cos{\theta} = x.  $ Define $Z^n+ \frac{1}{Z^n}= S_{n} \implies S_{0}= 2, S_{1}= x $. It is obvious that $S_{n}$ satisfies the recurrence

$$   S_{n+1} = x S_{n}- S_{n-1} $$

$$  \implies S_{2} = x^2-2, \ S_{3} = x^3 -3x, \ S_{4} = x^4-2x^2 +2, S_{5} = x^5-5x^3+5x.  $$

Substituting these results into $(B)$, we get

$$   x^5-x^4-4x^3+3x^2+3x-1= 0 $$

For every root $Z$ of equation $(A)$, equation $(B)$ is satisfied. Since each quantity

$$ Z^r+\frac1{Z^r} $$ is a polynomial in

$$ x=Z+\frac1Z, $$

every corresponding value of $x$ satisfies

$$ x^5-x^4-4x^3+3x^2+3x-1=0. $$

As this polynomial is of degree five, these are precisely all of its roots. Hence,

$$   x^5-x^4-4x^3+3x^2+3x-1 = \prod_{k=0}^{4} (x - 2 \cos{(\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{11})})  $$

Put $x=1$ to get $\alpha$.

$$  \implies 1 =  \prod_{k=0}^{4} (1 - 2 \cos{(\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{11})}) = \alpha $$

$$  \implies 5- \alpha^2 = 4. $$
