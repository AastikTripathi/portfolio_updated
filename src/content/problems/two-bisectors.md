## Problem Statement

In a triangle $ABC, L$ and $K$ are the points of intersections of angle bisectors of $\angle ABC $ and $ \angle BAC $ with segment $AC$ and $BC,$ respectively. The segment $KL$ is the angle bisector of $\angle BLC.$ Determine $ \angle ABC. $

## Solution & Proof

![](/images/problems/two-bisectors/Screenshot%202026-09-04%20at%206.15.53%E2%80%AFPM.png)

In the diagram above:\
\
Since $AK$ is the angle bisector of $\angle BAC,$ and $LK$ is the angle bisector of $ \angle BLC. $ Therefore, $K$ is the ex-centre of triangle of $ABL.$ Therefore, $BC$ is the angle bisector of the angle $\angle LBD.$
$$   \implies \angle LBC = \angle CBD $$
But we already have
$$  \angle ABL = \angle LBC   $$
And further we have
$$  \angle ABL + \angle LBC + \angle CBD = 180 ^\circ \implies \angle ABL = \angle LBC = \angle CBD = 60^\circ  $$
$$  \angle  ABC = 2 \angle ABL = 120 ^\circ $$
