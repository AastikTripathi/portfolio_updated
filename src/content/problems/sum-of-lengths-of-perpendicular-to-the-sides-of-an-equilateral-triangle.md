## Problem Statement

Prove that the sum of lengths of perpendicular from an interior point $P$ to the sides of an equilateral triangle is equal to the altitude of the equilateral triangle.

## Solution & Proof

![](/images/problems/sum-of-lengths-of-perpendicular-to-the-sides-of-an-equilateral-triangle/Screenshot%202026-08-29%20at%208.06.07%E2%80%AFPM.png)

Let $ABC$ be an equilateral triangle such that $P$ is an interior point. Let $p_{1}, p_{2}$ and $p_{3}$ be the lengths of perpendicular from $P$ upon $BC, AB$ and $CA$ respectively. Let the side length of the equilateral triangle be $a$. Since, we have:
$$  \text{area}(\Delta PBC ) +  \text{area}(\Delta PBA )+  \text{area}(\Delta PAC ) =  \text{area}(\Delta ABC )  $$
$$  \implies \frac{1}{2}(p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3})a = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2 $$
$$ \implies p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3} =  \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} a = \text{length of the altitude of the equilateral triangle.}  $$
